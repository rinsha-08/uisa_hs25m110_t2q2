Discussion

Temporal change

Built-up surface in Surat increased from 38.3 km² in 2000 to 60.3 km² in 2020, a rise of 22.0 km² (57%), or about 2.3% per year. Built-up land covered 9% of the ULB area in 2000 and 14% in 2020. The fastest interval was 2005–2010 (+7.2 km²).

Spatial pattern

The gross gain was 22.0 km² against a gross loss of 0.0 km². The NE and SW sectors together account for 41% of the growth, while the NW sector grew the least. The change map shows where new built-up cells appear relative to the 2000 footprint.

Limitations

  • Spatial resolution: GHSL uses a 100 m grid. Each cell gives built-up area in m² and not individual buildings, so narrow roads and small plots are blurred.
  • Temporal resolution: only 5-year epochs are available, so changes between epochs and the exact year of construction are not captured.
  • Modelled data: GHSL is derived from satellite imagery and modelling, and earlier epochs are less certain. Small decreases in some cells are modelling artefacts and not real demolition.
  • Boundary: the SBM municipal limit is used. Growth just outside it, and recent boundary extensions, are not captured.
  • Sectors: the compass wedges are a descriptive device around the polygon centroid, not official zones or wards.
